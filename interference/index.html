Guided experiment

Field guide: what each problem looks like
  • CW tone: one thin line and a steady waterfall stripe. IQ points become rings. Beacon normal.
  • Modulated signal: a raised block with width. IQ points become round clouds. Beacon normal.
  • Swept: a line moving across the waterfall, with errors on a repeating period.
  • Cross-pol: a signal much stronger on the opposite polarization. Every site if the uplink feed leaks, one site if its receive feed is skewed.
  • Adjacent satellite: one site only, usually a small or mispointed dish. It falls faster than your carrier rises when you repeak.
  • Local to one site: one site only, with the beacon normal. Check nearby emitters, cabling and the LNB.
  • Rain: one site, beacon and carrier fall together, and the noise floor rises.
  • Mispointing: one site, beacon and carrier fall together, and the noise floor stays flat.
  • Overdrive: every site and carrier, with shoulders beside the carriers and no new signal.
  • Power robbing: every site and carrier fall, with a strong new signal clear of both carriers.

Sources and further reading

Open references for the ideas in this lab. The simulator simplifies all of them.

Interference types operators see
Martin Coleman (sIRG), “Tackling Satellite Interference,” Microwave Journal, 2012. Cross-polarization, adjacent satellites, carriers that collapse to CW, carrier ID and geolocation.
sIRG, “Satellite Interference! Jamming, Carrier ID and Working Groups,” ITU workshop, Geneva, 2013 (PDF).
Intelsat General, “Adjacent Satellite Interference in Mobile/VSAT Environments,” white paper (Internet Archive copy, PDF). Why small and mispointed dishes see neighboring satellites.
Interference and jamming waveforms
Inside GNSS, “Listening for RF Noise”. Wideband, partial-band, narrowband, CW and swept interference, and how each looks on a spectrogram.
EMSOPEDIA, “Swept Jamming against Radio Communications” (Internet Archive copy, the site is offline).
Wikipedia, “Radio jamming”.
Keysight, “GNSS Jamming: How to Test the Risks to Safety-Critical and Liability-Critical Systems,” white paper (PDF). CW, chirp and pulsed jammer signals.
Edd Salkield, Sebastian Köhler, Simon Birnbach, Martin Strohmeier et al. (University of Oxford), “Space Jam: Protocol-aware Jamming Attacks against Space Communications,” 2025 (PDF). Compares noise and tone jamming with jamming that targets a protocol.
James Pavur, “Space Jam: Exploring Radio Frequency Attacks in Outer Space,” DEF CON 30, 2022. Jamming, spoofing and signal takeover.
Power robbing and polarization isolation
Robert Peters and Peter Woolner, “Analytic Calculation of Noise Power Robbing, NPR, and Polarization Isolation Degradation,” AIAA (NASA NTRS, PDF).
Transponder ALC
S.C. Bera, R.V. Singh and V.K. Garg (ISRO), “Design and Temperature Compensation of a Ku-band Channel Amplifier with ALC for a Satellite Transponder,” Microwave Journal, 2006.
Rain fade
Recommendation ITU-R P.618-14, Propagation data and prediction methods for Earth-space systems, 2023 (PDF).
IQ plots and MER
ETSI TR 101 290 V1.4.1, Measurement guidelines for DVB systems, 2020 (PDF).
Keysight, “Making and Interpreting EVM Measurements” (PDF).
Carrier ID
ETSI TS 103 129 V1.1.1, Carrier identification system (DVB-CID), 2013 (PDF).
Finding the source
Wikipedia, “Satellite geolocation”. How operators locate an uplink with two satellites.
Reporting and the law
ITU, “Preventing harmful radio interference”. Reporting through your regulator, and why jamming is prohibited.
