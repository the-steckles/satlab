Sources and further reading

Open references for the ideas in this lab. The simulator simplifies all of them.

Inspiration for this lab
James Pavur, “Space Jam: Exploring Radio Frequency Attacks in Outer Space,” DEF CON 30, 2022. The linked section covers taking over a DVB-S2 signal.
DVB-S2 frames, PLHEADER and MODCODs
ETSI EN 302 307-1 V1.4.1, the DVB-S2 standard, 2014 (PDF).
MER and monitoring
ETSI TR 101 290 V1.4.1, Measurement guidelines for DVB systems, 2020 (PDF).
Why total power barely moves
Robert Peters and Peter Woolner, “Analytic Calculation of Noise Power Robbing, NPR, and Polarization Isolation Degradation,” AIAA (NASA NTRS, PDF).
Content authentication
DVB-S2 does not authenticate what it carries, so the defense step shows the general idea. ATSC 3.0 broadcast TV is a real standard that signs its signaling and apps for the receiver to check.
ATSC A/360, “ATSC 3.0 Security and Service Protection”.
ATSC, “Security, Content Protection High Priorities for NEXTGEN TV”. Why broadcast signaling is signed.
Adrian Perrig, Ran Canetti, J. D. Tygar and Dawn Song, “Efficient Authentication and Signing of Multicast Streams over Lossy Channels,” 2000 (PDF). TESLA, a way to authenticate a one-way broadcast. RFC 4082 describes it.
EUSPA, “Galileo Open Service Navigation Message Authentication (OSNMA)”. A satellite broadcast that signs its data using TESLA.
Carrier ID
ETSI TS 103 129 V1.1.1, Carrier identification system (DVB-CID), 2013 (PDF).
A real takeover, before DVB-S2
Wikipedia, “Captain Midnight broadcast signal intrusion”. The 1986 analog overlay of HBO, and how the FCC traced it.
Finding the source and reporting it
Wikipedia, “Satellite geolocation”.
ITU, “Preventing harmful radio interference”.
Martin Coleman (sIRG), “Tackling Satellite Interference,” Microwave Journal, 2012.
